## CALL SCRIPT — If She Agrees to a 20-Minute Call

### Minute 0–2: Frame → "I appreciate your time. My goal for today is simple: I'm going to show you exactly what I found on dspr.in, you tell me if it's accurate, and we'll decide together if it's worth doing anything about it. Fair?"

*(Gets a "yes" early — commitment and consistency principle)*

### Minute 2–8: Live Screen Share of the Site
- Pull up dspr.in live
- Show the PageSpeed score (mobile) — make the number visible
- Walk the case study page: "Tell me when you feel the hook. You'll notice it comes late."
- Show the contact form: "If I were a CMO with a ₹50L budget and I just read your homepage, this is what I see next. How does that feel?"

*(Let her react — you want discomfort. Don't rush past it.)*

### Minute 8–12: Social Proof — Brief, Concrete, Similar
"I rebuilt a site for [Comparable Agency Name] in Q3. Their problem was exactly the same load time issue. Four weeks after the rebuid, they sent me this—" *(share a testimonial screenshot or metric)*

"Same industry. Similar brand positioning to DSPR. Same result."

*(Identity match: this is her world)*

### Minute 12–17: The Offer
"Here's what I'm proposing: I take on a fixed-scope rebuild of dspr.in. Not a redesign for redesign's sake — specifically targeting these three conversion bottlenecks. My estimate is X weeks, ₹Y investment.

The agencies I've done this for have typically seen that return covered in one or two new client sign-ons from improved inbound."

*(Anchor to ROI, not cost)*

### Minute 17–20: Handle Objections + Close

**"We have someone who manages our site."**  
→ "I work alongside internal teams all the time. What I'm bringing isn't code — it's an outside conversion perspective that your current team doesn't have the bandwidth to audit objectively. We wouldn't replace anyone."

**"Send me a proposal."**  
→ "Absolutely. Before I do, let me make sure I scope it correctly — can I ask you two questions so the proposal is specific to DSPR's goals and not a generic document?"
*(Re-engage the conversation. Generic proposals die in inboxes.)*

**"What's the investment?"**  
→ "For a project of this scope, typically ₹[range]. But I don't quote until I know the brief completely — I don't want to give you a number that's too high because I overestimated, or too low because I missed something. That's why I'd want to send a scoped proposal after this call."

**"I'll think about it."**  
→ "Of course. Can I ask — what would need to be true for this to be a yes? I'd rather know now if there's something I haven't addressed than follow up blind."

## PSYCHOLOGICAL PRINCIPLES APPLIED

| Principle | Where Applied |
|---|---|
| **Pain amplification before solution** | Email 1 — lead with 3 specific problems, no solution offered yet |
| **Social proof (similar other)** | Email 1 + Call: comparable agency, specific result |
| **Loss aversion / competitor FOMO** | Email 3 — competitor just moved; position is being taken |
| **Reciprocity** | Free audit video — creates obligation to respond |
| **Scarcity** | "One agency per city per vertical" — genuine or implied exclusivity |
| **Commitment ladder** | Call opens with a small "fair?" — first yes is free; each next yes is easier |
| **Identity match** | Mirror her language, her world, her competitive context |
| **Specificity bias** | "6.2 seconds," "38% increase," "8 weeks" — vague claims are ignored; numbers are believed |
| **Breakup email** | Creates finality; triggers fear of missed opportunity in people who were "thinking about it" |
